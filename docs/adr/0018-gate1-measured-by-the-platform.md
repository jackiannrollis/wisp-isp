# ADR 0018 · Gate 1 measured by the platform

**Status:** Accepted
**Date:** 2026-10-27 (amended the same day with the user's changes: restores by kind, manual reconnects recorded directly, alerting by severity, Q18)

## Context

Gate 1 (docs/09) is the end of v1: the pilot ISP completes two billing cycles with no manual reconnects, payment matching above 98% automatic, and no restore slower than 60 seconds after the callback. The pilot-onboarding runbook measured it with three SQL approximations run by hand on the host. Two were weak: the restore time joined a matched payment to the last CoA of any of the customer's lines (`network_line_coa` keeps only the last one, so a line restored twice counted once, and `matched_at` is not when the callback arrived), and matching counted manual cash entries as staff-matched. A line with no live session at the moment it is restored gets no Disconnect at all (AccessChanges: its next login is full from the view), so "the router acknowledged" cannot be the only end of a restore. Manual reconnects had no record at all: staff could only kick a session on the router itself.

## Decision

- **The reporting module records the facts from events.** `CreditApplied` (subscriptions) gives a payment that renewed lines, each with its access profile and reason just before (`previousProfile`, `previousReason`). A line the payment lifted back to full speed becomes a row of `reporting_restore` when the payment arrived by M-Pesa callback, with its **kind**: `CUT_OFF` (RESTRICTED: expired past grace, or a data cap), `GRACE_THROTTLE` or `FAIR_USE` (THROTTLED). A first activation (REJECT before) is not a restore. `callback_at` is when the platform first stored a callback for that payment (`payments_mpesa_callback.received_at`, P2), not M-Pesa's `TransTime`; `renewed_at` is the renewal. `SessionReauthorized` (network) is kept in `reporting_reauthorization`, because it may arrive before the credit's event. Consumers are idempotent (E2).
- **A restore is the platform's part done**, at the first of: the renewal itself when the line had no live session then (`NO_SESSION`: no Disconnect is sent, the next login is full); the router answering the Disconnect or CoA (`REAUTHORIZED`) or saying the session had already ended (`SESSION_GONE`); a new session of the line (`NEW_SESSION`, from `radacct`, which also covers a router that never answered while the customer redialled). Only what happens within 10 minutes of the renewal counts; a restore still open after that is **missed**. The same rules and the 60-second threshold apply to every kind. A job completes open restores of the last 7 days every minute (and every read does first), so the order of events never matters and a job outage shorter than that loses nothing.
- **Matching** counts payments that arrived by callback (any `payments_mpesa_callback` row points at them): automatic is `MATCHED` without a staff assignment, by staff is `MATCHED` with one, and `UNMATCHED` is still in suspense. Manual payments are not matching and are left out. "Above 98%" is judged on the counts (automatic × 100 > total × 98); the percentage shown is rounded to one decimal.
- **Manual reconnects are recorded directly** in `reporting_manual_reconnect`, with the actor (and the platform admin when Corebalt support acted while impersonating) and a reason:
  - `STAFF_DISCONNECT`: the admin app's new "Disconnect sessions" on a line (`POST /api/v1/subscriptions/{id}/disconnect`, `subscriptions.write`, a reason, an `Idempotency-Key`; audited, Disconnect through the worker as N5, `LineDisconnectedByStaff`), counted when it comes within 2 hours after the customer's latest matched payment (`matched_at`, any channel);
  - `PAID_OFFLINE`: support's "Paid but still offline" on the customer page (`POST /api/v1/subscriptions/{id}/paid-offline-reports`, `customers.write`, an optional note; audited), an incident on the line linked to the customer's latest payment whenever it was (409 when the customer never paid).
  The criterion "no manual reconnects" counts both, the missed restores, and, kept from the first version, the `COA_FAILED` and `ROUTER_OFFLINE` staff alerts of the period.
- **Where it shows:** `GET /api/platform/tenants/{id}/gate1?days=1..90` (PLATFORM tokens only; default 30) and the console's tenant page (60 days by default; restores overall and by kind; each criterion met, not met, or nothing to judge yet). Gauges per tenant, window (last hour, last 30 days) and kind, every minute: `isp_gate1_restores`, `isp_gate1_restores_slow`, `isp_gate1_restores_missed`, `isp_gate1_restore_p95_seconds`, `isp_gate1_manual_reconnects`, `isp_gate1_matching_automatic_ratio`; two tables on the Platform dashboard (by tenant, by tenant and kind).
- **Alerting:** `CorebaltGate1RestoreSlow` (warning, at once) for a restore slower than 60 seconds in the last hour; `CorebaltGate1RestoreMissed` (critical) for a missed restore, because a paid customer may still be cut off; `CorebaltGate1RestoresSlowRepeated` (critical) for three or more slow restores of one tenant within an hour, which inhibits the warning for that tenant. Critical goes to the operators' route like every critical alert (email and the webhook, repeated every 4 hours); there is no separate pager.
- **Visibility (Q18, resolved):** Gate 1 itself, pass or fail and its thresholds, stays platform-only. A tenant-facing "Collections health" card (automatically matched share, median restore time, the unmatched queue, without gate wording) is on the v2 backlog (docs/09).

## Consequences

- Gate 1 numbers exist from this release on; no pilot ran before it, so the runbook's SQL approximations are gone. Manual payments count for neither matching nor restores, so the two billing cycles count from the pilot's Daraja go-live.
- The measure is the platform's part. A customer whose equipment takes a minute to redial after the Disconnect is restored at the router's answer; that time is not measured.
- A staff "Disconnect sessions" inside a restore's 10 minutes completes that restore (the router's answer cannot be told from the platform's own Disconnect), so the restore may look fast; the manual reconnect it records still fails the criterion.
- The missed-restore alert covers the last hour: it resolves an hour after the restore was missed whether or not the customer is back. A missed restore stays missed in Gate 1.
- A reconnect done on the router itself (Winbox) still leaves no trace; the pilot's staff are asked to use the admin app's action or the "Paid but still offline" button instead (runbook step 7).
- `reporting_reauthorization` grows by one row per CoA or Disconnect; nothing purges it yet (a few thousand rows a month per pilot-sized tenant).
