# ADR 0014 · Cutover import: an imported line activated with the expiry the customer already paid for

**Status:** Accepted (Q16 resolved 2026-10-26; option A with the changes under Decision)
**Date:** 2026-10-26

## Context

A line is `PENDING` until its first renewal is paid (docs/04; S5 renews from the moment of payment when a line is past grace; B5). The CSV import of a tenant's customers (docs/07 step 4) opened `PENDING` lines only. An ISP moving existing, paying customers onto the platform therefore had two ways to cut over (`infra/ops/runbooks/pilot-onboarding.md`, Customers): per customer on their renewal date, which means a rolling month with the old system running beside the platform, or on one day with a manual payment per customer, which gives every customer a fresh cycle and gives away the days they had already paid for in the old system.

Two more things a lossless cutover depends on were missing from the import. Customer routers are configured with the old system's PPPoE username and password, and new credentials would mean touching every router; the import generated both, and the username's shape and platform-wide uniqueness (T2) left no room for an old name. And customers keep paying with their old M-Pesa account numbers for months; matching (P3) knew only the customer number, and its normalisation turns a digits-only old reference into somebody else's short customer number.

What existed: `activated_at`, `cycle_started_at`, `grace_days` and `grace_mode` on the line; `SubscriptionStateChanged` with the causes `STAFF_ACTION`, `RENEWAL` and `AUTO_RESUME_RECORDED`; an import that validates every row or none; the audit trail (A1); FreeRADIUS asking the view for a username together with the asking router's address, and the accounting trigger resolving the tenant from the router before the username (T3).

## Options

**A. The import may activate a line with its current expiry (chosen).** A CSV column `paid_until`, optional per row. A row that has it opens the line `ACTIVE` at import: `expires_at` = `paid_until`, `activated_at` = now, `cycle_started_at` = `paid_until` minus the package duration (so fair use counts the right cycle, N9), grace days and mode copied from the tenant's policy (S6). No ledger entry: the old system collected that money, and the wallet starts empty. An audit row per line, and `SubscriptionStateChanged` (`PENDING` to `ACTIVE`) with a new cause `IMPORTED`, which no consumer acts on: there is no session to change. A `paid_until` in the past is allowed: the line is `ACTIVE` and expired, so the customer lands on the pay page (`RESTRICTED`, S2) instead of being rejected.

**B. Keep the rule.** The runbook's two cutovers stay as they are. Rejected: a rolling cutover or a cutover that gives away paid time.

**C. A "cutover payment".** A manual payment flagged as settling the old cycle, activating until a given date instead of one duration ahead. Rejected: it records a money movement that did not happen on the platform (B1, B3), numbers a receipt for money the old system took (B7) and bends P4 and P5 for a one-time migration.

## Decision

Option A, with these changes to the proposal:

1. **A cutover window instead of a permanent power.** `paid_until` is accepted only while the tenant's cutover window is open. A Corebalt platform admin opens it (`PUT /api/platform/tenants/{id}/cutover-window`) with an end at most 30 days ahead; it closes by itself, or earlier by `DELETE`. Both audit logs record it; the tenant sees it in its account settings. The permission is a dedicated one, `subscriptions.import_active`, held by the built-in Owner role only (the role editor refuses it, like `settings.write`) and requiring 2FA (A2), not `payments.record_manual`: Finance holds that one, and granting cycles is a different power. The "free cycle for any new customer" risk is bounded to onboarding.
2. **Preview before commit.** The import has a dry run (`POST /api/v1/customer-imports/preview`) with the same validation and nothing written. It returns the lines activating, already expired and in the future; the earliest and latest `paid_until`; the value of the time granted (the package price pro rata of the time still ahead, B6 rounding); warnings when more than 10% of the dated rows are in the past or, with at least ten dated rows, more than 90% fall on days 1 to 12 (both usually mean DD/MM read as MM/DD), when old references are 1 to 6 digits (they shadow the short form of customer numbers in P3) and when activating rows keep no credentials; and a confirmation, a digest of the file and its options. An import with `paid_until` must send that confirmation back, so the summary was seen for the very file committed. Committing writes one batch audit row with those totals plus the per-line rows, and keeps the batch (`subscriptions_import_batch`, its rows) so the "we've moved" SMS can reach exactly its customers.
3. **Date-only values.** `paid_until` accepts `YYYY-MM-DD` as well as an ISO 8601 date-time. A date alone means a time of day in the tenant's zone, chosen on the import (`timeOfDay`, default 12:00), so the expiry lands in daytime, when customers can pay and staff can help, not at midnight. `DD/MM/YYYY` is refused, never guessed.
4. **Kept PPPoE credentials.** Columns `pppoe_username` and `pppoe_password`, both or neither, only with a package. The line keeps them (`username_kept`); the password is written through `LineCredentialStore.keep` and never returned, logged, audited by value or echoed in an error. T2 is widened: a kept name is 1 to 64 of rlm_sql's safe characters without space, colon or slash, is unique within its tenant (the platform-wide unique constraint on usernames went; a plain index stays for the view's lookups), and is refused when it matches the generated shape, so generated names stay unique across the platform and the two namespaces never meet. The view needed nothing: it already lists a line once per router of its own tenant and FreeRADIUS filters by username and the packet's source address (T3); proven against the real FreeRADIUS image with the same name in two lab tenants. A kept name typed wrong is corrected from the line (`PUT /api/v1/subscriptions/{id}/pppoe-username`, the same permission) while the window is open; a generated name never changes. The credential table's length floor applies to router secrets only: a kept password may be as short as the old system allowed.
5. **Old M-Pesa references.** `legacy_account_ref` on the customer, from the import or the customer page: trimmed, upper-cased, unique per tenant, 1 to 32 letters, digits, spaces and `. _ / # -`. P3 gains an exact step right after the customer number and before normalisation, because a digits-only old reference would otherwise read as the short form of a customer number that exists after the import. The reverse risk, a post-cutover customer typing the short form of their new number and hitting someone's old reference, is why the preview warns about digit-only references and the runbook says to clear old references after two cycles. A one-time "we've moved" SMS (`MOVED_NOTICE`, `MOVED_NOTICE_LEGACY` with the old reference; tenant-editable, new placeholder `legacy_ref`) goes to an import batch when staff ask (`POST /api/v1/notifications/moved-notices`), once per customer and batch (M1), in the sending window (M2).
6. **The lossless one-day cutover** is the runbook's third cutover, in this order: the operator opens the window and the Owner rehearses the preview on the export; SMS is switched off for the tenant; the final export is imported; each router's PPPoE server is pointed at the platform's profile and its old RADIUS entry disabled, sessions cleared in batches, the auth log watched; M-Pesa callbacks are registered on the platform (or, before go-live, Finance records payments here); SMS is switched on and the notice sent. SMS stays off between the import and the callback switch because reminders would otherwise ask customers to pay with their new number before those payments could reach the platform.

Rule **S11** records the activation; T2, P3 and M1 are amended; docs/04, 05, 06, 07 and the runbook describe it.

## Consequences

- An Owner could grant any new customer a free cycle at import time, but only while Corebalt has opened the window, at most one cycle, after a preview, and with every activation and the batch's totals in the audit log.
- The platform's own metering (Q1) counts these lines as active subscribers from the import, which is right: they are served.
- The first renewal of an imported line follows S5 from its `paid_until`, so the customer's billing day is preserved across the migration, which is the point.
- Logins do not change for generated names. A kept name is matched exactly as the router sends it, case included; PAP and CHAP both work from the kept password; a customer device that insists on MS-CHAPv2 would fail, which stays unproven until the network lab runs on real routers.
- No `Idempotency-Key` on the import, as before: its response carries show-once passwords, which must not be replayable, and a replay fails validation on the duplicate phones.
- A customer who still pays with an old reference months later is matched; one who types the short form of a new number that equals someone's old reference is matched to the old one until staff clear it.
